# Pitfalls Research

**Domain:** Child-focused digital-safety / healthy-screen-time education website (German, ages 10–12, PWA-style static site)
**Researched:** 2026-09-25
**Confidence:** MEDIUM-HIGH (health claims & iOS/EU facts cross-checked across multiple independent sources; free-host limit figures from 2026 vendor comparisons)

## Critical Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Fear-mongering and moral-panic framing ("Handysucht", "digitale Demenz")

**What goes wrong:**
The site slides into scare-tactics content — "your phone is rewiring your brain", "you're addicted", horror statistics about dopamine. For a 10–12 year old the effect is the opposite of the goal: fear-based, abstinence-based messaging makes kids tune out, feel guilty, or reject the message entirely. A 120-expert review of 1,400 studies found strong causal evidence only for sleep disruption and behavioral addiction — *no* direct causal link between general screen/social-media use and mental-health decline. The word "addiction" itself is contested: researchers prefer "problematic media use". Framing the whole site around "Sucht" positions a normal childhood activity as a disease — and the child as the patient.

**Why it happens:**
"Dangers of smartphone overuse" is the project's stated entry point, and fear headlines are what adults find compelling ("Handy macht krank" sells). The parent-author world is saturated with moral-panic media (Spitzer-style "digitale Demenz" books, scandalized news coverage). It takes deliberate effort to write facts-first instead of fright-first.

**How to avoid:**
- Adopt "facts + agency" as a written editorial rule: every danger claim must be paired with a concrete, doable alternative ("Your eyes get tired from staring close-up — try the 20-20-20 rule").
- Use graded language: "kann dazu führen" / "das ist ein Zeichen, dass..." instead of "du bist süchtig".
- Let the child be the hero: self-check questions framed as curiosity ("Interessiert dich, wie gut deine Augen-Pausen klappen?") not diagnosis ("Bist du süchtig? Teste dich!").
- Cite the science lightly and honestly (even 1–2 small "Woher wissen wir das?" expanders build trust far more than alarm).

**Warning signs:**
- Draft contains words like "Sucht", "Abhängigkeit", "zerstört", "gefährlich", "verboten", "Leiden" far more often than "Balance", "Pause", "ohne Handy auch schön".
- Any "Teste dich!" quiz that can return a verdict ("Du bist süchtig") — grades are shaming, not informing.
- Content only lists harms and never mentions that screens are also great (friends, music, learning).

**Phase to address:** Phase 1 (Content – all six topics). Review gate: a tone checklist applied to every topic page before it ships.

### Pitfall 2: The parent-lecture voice (owner IS the parent)

**What goes wrong:**
The author is the father. The single biggest risk is the site reading like a father lecture ("Du sollst nicht so viel am Handy hängen", "Weißt du eigentlich, wie schlecht das für dich ist?"). A 10–12 year old will hear the parent's voice in it instantly, feel lectured-to, and close it. The project explicitly targets "friendly guide, never a lecture" — but this is the default failure mode for exactly this author.

**Why it happens:**
Parents write to kids the way they talk to kids — with authority, correction, and concern. The concern is real and correct, which makes the urge to "set rules through the site" almost irresistible. The child's perceived need (autonomy, being treated like she's growing up) is why it backfires.

**How to avoid:**
- Write in the third person / neutral guide voice, or even first-person plural ("Wir schauen uns heute an, was im Gehirn beim Scrollen passiert") — never "Du MUSST".
- Prefer suggestions over commands: "Probiere aus: Handy weglegen 30 Minuten vor dem Schlafen – was merkst du?" over "Legen Sie das Handy weg".
- Include the good stuff prominently: what's awesome about phones, games, and social contact — the site must feel like it's on her side, not policing her.
- Have a non-parent reviewer (spouse, teacher, an actual 10–12 year old) read drafts: if it "sounds like a parent wrote it", rewrite.
- Never use the site to enforce family rules. Family rules belong to the family, not to the website.

**Warning signs:**
- Drafting in the imperative "Du sollst/du musst/du darfst nicht".
- Topics that map 1:1 to household arguments ("Du räumst dein Handy nicht weg beim Essen...").
- Content that reads like a list of complaints the parent has.

**Phase to address:** Phase 1 (Content + tone pass) and re-checked in Phase 2 (interactive elements — quiz/tip wording must also stay non-lecture).

### Pitfall 3: Inaccurate or overstated health claims (eyes, posture, addiction science)

**What goes wrong:**
The site claims things that pediatric evidence does not support, and the daughter (or her friends' parents) later catches it — destroying the site's credibility. Specifically:
- **Eyes/blue light:** AAP: *no evidence* that blue light from normal digital screens damages children's eyes, and blue-light glasses are generally not needed. "Screens ruin your eyes" is false; eye *strain* from prolonged near-focusing is real.
- **Myopia:** myopia risk is associated with prolonged near-work AND reduced outdoor time (outdoor time is protective) — it's about displacement, not the screen itself. Claiming "Handys verursachen Kurzsichtigkeit" is an overstatement.
- **Sleep:** this is the *best-supported* claim — screen light + cognitive arousal near bedtime delay melatonin and sleep onset. Lean on this one; it's truthful and kid-relatable.
- **Posture:** hunched posture causes strain and discomfort; "permanent back damage" claims are weakly supported. "Doomscrolling couch potato" framing is metaphor, not science.
- **Dopamine/"likes-dopamine":** pop-science oversimplification. Research (120 experts, 1,400 studies) found strong evidence of *behavioral addiction patterns* in some teens and sleep harm — not a clean "dopamine hit" mechanistic story. Keep "Wumms im Kopf" as an analogy for kids, never as a proven fact.

**Why it happens:**
Well-meaning adults recycle media-friendly health claims without checking the pediatric literature; "sounds plausible and scary" is indistinguishable from true to a lay author. German child-health sites and viral articles are full of these half-truths, so models repeating them feel "safe".

**How to avoid:**
- Build a small fact sheet per topic before writing (what's well-supported / partially supported / myth) using AAP, Mayo Clinic, and pediatric-sleep sources — the site then only uses "facts that pediatricians would sign".
- Use hedged language for association-level claims: "Kinder, die viel draußen sind, haben seltener Kurzsichtigkeit" (fact) vs "Handys machen kurzsichtig" (unsupported).
- Prefer mechanism-based, actionable facts (sleep, breaks, stretching) over disease claims (addiction diagnosis, eye damage).
- Add a "Was stimmt – was nicht?" myth-buster section; it's a differentiator and inoculation against misinformation elsewhere.

**Warning signs:**
- Any draft sentence containing "zerstört", "schädigt das Gehirn", "macht süchtig", "verursacht" followed by a body part.
- No source discipline: every claim should be checkable against one of the trusted pediatric sources.

**Phase to address:** Phase 1 (Content). Prevention = a facts-check step in the content pipeline; verification = a "facts review" checklist item with the two or three trusted sources named.

### Pitfall 4: Age-inappropriate language and reading level (German for 10–12)

**What goes wrong:**
Texts are written at adult comprehension level (long nested clauses, abstract nouns, medical jargon, "Nominalstil") — or, oppositely, at preschool level (baby-talk, childish illustrations that make her feel patronized). NN/G research is blunt: there is no "children" as one audience; 9–12 ("older children") *scan* pages, need clear structure, and reject anything that feels babyish. German is harder here: umlauts, ''ß'', compound nouns and passive voice make adult-formal German almost unreadable to a 10-year-old.

**Why it happens:**
Adults write the way they read (adult news, Behörden-German, medical pamphlets). Nobody measures readability because it "sounds fine to me". The owner's daughter is one child — but the site is explicitly shareable ("mit Freundinnen teilen"), and classmates may be weaker readers.

**How to avoid:**
- Target a concrete reading rule: sentences ≤ 12 words, one idea per sentence, active voice, concrete nouns, explain every technical word the first time ("Melatonin – das ist das Hormon, das müde macht").
- Use a German readability check (there are tools for LIX / "einfache Sprache"); aim for the levels used by "logo!" or "Dein Spiegel" (Kinder-Nachrichten) rather than adult news.
- Format for scanning: short paragraphs, bullet lists, bold key words, emoji-sparse (a little is fine), illustrative examples from her world (TikTok-Videos, Klassenchat, GTA, Lesen unterm Bett).
- Never talk down: no "ganz liebe Kinder" fluff. 10–12 year olds smell condescension instantly.

**Warning signs:**
- Average sentence length > 15 words; any word a 10-year-old couldn't explain (Hormon, Regulation, Konzentration un-chunked).
- Draft is a wall of text with no subheadings/breaks.

**Phase to address:** Phase 1 (Content) with a readability pass per topic; verify in testing (Phase: launch validation) with the actual daughter and ideally one classmate.

### Pitfall 5: Self-check questions that grade, judge, or shame

**What goes wrong:**
Interactive "self-check" questions become a test with right/wrong verdicts, scores, streaks, or a final judgment ("Du bist stark gefährdet. Du solltest das Handy abgeben."). For an anxious or sensitive 10–12 year old this is actively harmful — it converts education into evaluation and can cement a "something is wrong with me" self-story. Gamification pressure (timers, points, leaderboards) raises stress and is cited as a fairness/engagement pitfall even in classroom literature.

**Why it happens:**
Quizzes feel like "interactive" so builders make them scored; scoring is also the lazy way to "show learning". The project's stated format ("kleine Selbstcheck-Fragen") invites this trap.

**How to avoid:**
- Design questions as *reflection prompts*, not tests: "Woran merkst du, dass deine Augen müde werden?" with checkboxes, no wrong answers.
- Never give a verdict or score; give *gentle mirrors* ("Klingt so, als hätten deine Augen manchmal Pause nötig – hier ist eine Idee dazu.").
- Tip boxes ("Was kann ich tun?") must be suggestions with choice, phrased as invitations, not instructions.
- If fun scoring is irresistible, score progress *through the site* (topics explored), never the child's habits.

**Warning signs:**
- Any quiz logic with a "danger level" / percent score / "you are addicted" outcome.
- Copy uses "richtig/falsch" or implies the child could *fail*.

**Phase to address:** Phase 2 (Interactive elements). Wording review gate for every question: non-judgmental, no verdicts.

### Pitfall 6: One-sided "screens are bad" message (ignores that screens are also great)

**What goes wrong:**
A 10–12 year old (digital native) knows phones are wonderful — friends, games, creativity, music. If the site only lists dangers, it's instantly dismissed as wrong/naive; credibility dies and the real lessons (balance, sleep, body awareness) get thrown out with the "grown-up nagging". Seattle Children's / Mayo guidance itself emphasizes healthy relationships *with* media, not abstinence.

**Why it happens:**
The brief ("Gefahren der Smartphone-Übernutzung") points at dangers. It's easier and more dramatic to write dangers; balance requires more thought. The site's own "facts first, gentle encouragement" goal is the fix — it just needs enforcing.

**How to avoid:**
- Give every topic a "Was ist daran eigentlich gut?" section (screen time → learning, connecting; sleep → why dreams matter; body → what your body is for and noticing it feels good).
- Frame the goal as *balance and self-awareness*, never *reduction as the goal*.
- Use positive, competence-building framing for alternatives ("Was du stattdessen kannst") rather than negative ("Hör auf...").

**Warning signs:**
- Every topic page ends with a warning; zero pages end with an invitation to enjoy something.
- A child reader would describe the site's message as "Handys sind schlecht".

**Phase to address:** Phase 1 (Content). Balance check in the same tone-review gate.

### Pitfall 7: Assuming "home-screen app" just works (iOS/EU PWA reality)

**What goes wrong:**
The site is published, dad says "add it to your home screen", and: on iOS-Safari there is **no automatic install prompt** — the path is hidden (Share sheet → "Add to Home Screen"), and most people never find it. Worse, in iOS 17.4 (Feb 2024) Apple *removed* home-screen web apps in the EU citing the Digital Markets Act, then **reversed** in March 2024 after outcry — PWAs still work, but the status quo is unstable, EU users got degraded standalone behavior, and features like push/badges are unreliable. On Android-Chrome install is easy (banner + "Installieren"); iOS needs hand-holding. A child opening a broken half-installed "app" (generic globe icon, no standalone window) perceives it as broken and stops using it.

**Why it happens:**
"PWA installieren" is trivial to say and platform-specific to do. Most PWA tutorials are Android-first; iOS (the likely device for a 10–12 year old in Germany — iPhones dominate) contradicts the happy path. The DMA saga makes this a moving target even for specialists.

**How to avoid:**
- Plan a dedicated **"Installieren aufs Handy" page at the very start of hosting phase** with step-by-step instructions + screenshots for BOTH iOS (Share → Add to Home Screen; verify "Öffnen als Web-App" if using iOS 26 "Save as web app") and Android (Chrome-Menü → "App installieren").
- Order the first deployment before heavy content work: validate install on the real target phone (check which OS the daughter actually has!) immediately.
- Verify manifest correctness early (Pitfall 8) so the first install attempt succeeds.
- Do **not** rely on a service worker, push notifications, or offline caching — none are needed for a facts site and iOS support is the flakiest part of PWAs on iPhones. "App feel" = icon on home screen + standalone window; that's it.

**Warning signs:**
- No install-instructions page exists in the roadmap.
- Nobody has tested "Add to Home Screen" on the daughter's actual phone model/OS.
- Design assumes push notifications or offline mode will exist.

**Phase to address:** Hosting/Launch phase (install instructions + first-device validation), fed by Phase on PWA manifest.

### Pitfall 8: Broken/partial web-manifest and icon setup (generic globe icon)

**What goes wrong:**
Without a correct `manifest.json` (name, short_name, start_url, display, icons, theme_color) and `apple-touch-icon`/favicon set, iOS/Android install produces a generic gray globe — the "app" looks like a broken bookmark, and a child loses trust/interest ("Sieht kaputt aus"). Manifest parsing is picky: wrong icon `sizes`, missing 192×192/512×512 entries, wrong `sizes` vs actual pixel dimensions, missing `purpose:"maskable"` context for Android — all cause icons to render wrong. iOS particularly wants an `apple-touch-icon` (180×180) and correct `apple-touch-startup-image`.

**Why it happens:**
Manifest is invisible plumbing; it's the last thing people test and the first thing that breaks cross-platform. Icon generation without checking real rendered output (squircle mask on iOS, circle on Android) ships "it looks right in the editor" bugs.

**How to avoid:**
- Treat manifest + icons as a dedicated, testable deliverable: generate 512/192/180/32 icons from one source SVG, verify `sizes` attributes match actual pixels.
- Check the install result visually on BOTH iOS and Android in the install-validation step (same gate as Pitfall 7).
- Use `display: "standalone"`, `theme_color` matching the design, `background_color` to avoid white flash on launch.
- Keep the manifest one level deep with correct relative paths regardless of hosting subpath (classic GitHub Pages `https://user.github.io/repo/` root-relative bug!).

**Warning signs:**
- No manifest present yet in any plan; icons are not part of a deliverable.
- Icon file list lacks 512 px and 180 px variants.
- `start_url`/icons written as absolute paths that would break under a subpath.

**Phase to address:** PWA "App feel" phase (manifest/icons as explicit tasks) + validated in Hosting/Launch install gate.

### Pitfall 9: Content updates never reach the installed app (cache staleness)

**What goes wrong:**
The parent edits content ("I added a new tip!"), deploys, and the installed home-screen app still shows the old version — iOS web apps and service-workers cache aggressively; without cache-busting (versioned filenames, `no-cache` on HTML, purging) the daughter's phone keeps serving stale content for weeks. For a living, parent-maintained site this kills trust ("Dad's site is outdated").

**Why it happens:**
Static hosts set long cache headers; service workers default to cache-first; iOS home-screen web-app storage is opaque. "Just merge to main and it's deployed" gives perfect behavior in the parent's desktop browser and stale behavior on the phone — classic "works on my machine".

**How to avoid:**
- If using a service worker: version it, use network-first for HTML, and never cache-first un-hashed assets. Simpler: **skip the service worker entirely** (not needed for an installable facts site) and rely on host cache headers only.
- Use hashed/versioned asset filenames for CSS/JS/images so only HTML revalidates.
- Test the update path explicitly: change a headline → deploy → check the installed app reflects it (part of the install-validation gate).
- Keep a small "Datum der letzten Aktualisierung" on the site so staleness is visible and harmless.

**Warning signs:**
- Long `Cache-Control` headers on HTML pages in the deploy config.
- A service worker exists that isn't versioned and does cache-first for the landing page.
- The validation checklist has no "after update, installed app shows new content" step.

**Phase to address:** Hosting/Launch phase (cache headers/CDN config) + explicit update-path test.

### Pitfall 10: Free-static-host limits & privacy mismatch (public repo of a real child's site)

**What goes wrong:**
- **GitHub Pages free = public repository only.** Hosting a site about a real, identifiable 10–12 year old child (name, details, possibly photos) in a public repo means *the entire source, including any private notes or metadata, is world-readable forever*. The alternative (private repo + Pages) is paid.
- Host-count limits can bite: Netlify/Vercel cap free bandwidth ~100 GB/mo (irrelevant at this scale, but Netlify added credit-based billing in Sept 2025 and pauses sites that exceed free-tier limits); build-minutes on Netlify (300/mo); GitHub Pages 10 builds/hr soft limit (bypassable via Actions).
- Data residency: GitHub Pages/Netlify serve from US infrastructure/CDNs; combined with any tracking (see Pitfall 11) that's data leaving the EU. Static files with no personal data → legally fine, but the "comfortable" net effect should be decided knowingly.

**Why it happens:**
GitHub Pages is the default recommendation ("free + easy"), and nobody realizes the public-repo implication until after a child's real name is in a commit forever. "Free hosting" lists rarely mention privacy tradeoffs.

**How to avoid:**
- Choose the host for **repo/account privacy first**: Cloudflare Pages and Netlify free tiers support private Git repos (build from private repo) — better fit for a child-focused site. Decide: do we want the source public at all?
- Or keep GitHub Pages but enforce a hard rule: **zero identifying information in the repo** — no real name, school, town, photos with EXIF, classmate names. Use a site pseudonym ("HandyChecker" persona) + Impressum-safe minimal data (see Pitfall 11).
- Estimate scale honestly: this site will never approach 100 GB/mo; bandwidth caps are a non-issue. The *privacy* constraint is the real selection criterion.
- For EU/German users, Cloudflare Pages additionally has EU edge + no bandwidth cap — reasonable default if domain is simple.

**Warning signs:**
- Roadmap says "GitHub Pages" without addressing repo visibility.
- Repo would naturally contain screenshots/drafts with the daughter's real name.
- No decision recorded on public-vs-private repo before first commit.

**Phase to address:** Hosting/Launch phase (host selection with privacy criteria + repo hygiene rules). If private-repo host chosen, negligible rework risk; if GitHub Pages, repo hygiene becomes mandatory.

### Pitfall 11: Legal/compliance blind spots for a German child-facing site

**What goes wrong:**
Two failure modes: (a) **over-compliance panic** — adding cookie banners, tracking consent walls, and account systems where none are needed (there is no personal data; a cookie banner on a data-free static site is noise that erodes trust); (b) **under-compliance** — adding cute-but-tracking widgets (Google Fonts, analytics, share buttons, comment widgets) that silently process data from children and trigger GDPR duties; and forgetting that **German law requires an Impressum** for any site that is not purely private (the line: "geschäftsmäßig"). Germany's consent age for online services is 16 (Art. 8 GDPR) — anything collecting data from under-16s needs parental consent. If the site ever adds a form, newsletter, or "like" feature, the compliance surface grows instantly.

**Why it happens:**
"A tiny personal site needs nothing" (false once any third-party trackers are embedded) and "it's just an app icon" (false — it's a website subject to the same rules). Free-host templates sneak in analytics; copy-templates sneak in cookie banners.

**How to avoid:**
- **Zero-tracking design rule**: no analytics, no web-font CDNs, no platform embeds; self-host fonts or use system fonts; static site = no cookies at all. If there are no cookies/analytics, a cookie banner is *wrong* — don't add one.
- Add a simple **Impressum + Datenschutzerklärung** page: even if arguably not required for a purely private family site, it costs 30 minutes, covers the "friendlier/visible" edge once friends' parents see it, and is reachable in ≤2 clicks from every page. Use minimal data (Vorname/none, E-Mail-Kontakt) — do NOT publish the child's real name; publish the parent's.
- If anything interactive that *stores* input (quiz answers aren't personal data if not transmitted; but a comment form would be) is ever considered → that's a new phase requiring consent logic per Art. 8 GDPR (parental consent, age screen) — flag it, don't wing it.
- German content: keep the Impressum and privacy text in German, plain-language, and one click's reach visible.

**Warning signs:**
- Any planned embed/font/analytics snippet in the tech stack.
- No Impressum page in the roadmap at all.
- Someone proposes a cookie banner (leads the child to think the site tracks her).

**Phase to address:** Hosting/Launch phase (Impressum + Datenschutz page as explicit deliverable); "no third-party tracking" as an architecture rule from Phase 1.

### Pitfall 12: Mobile UX failures specific to a child's phone use (tiny targets, no back button, text walls)

**What goes wrong:**
- The installed app runs in a standalone window: **there is no browser back button and no URL bar** — if the site doesn't provide its own back/navigation ("zurück" buttons), the child gets stuck on a page and gives up.
- Touch targets sized for adult precision (44 px minimum on iOS): a 10–12 year old (smaller fingers, less precise) needs generously sized, well-spaced taps; tiny links are a guaranteed source of frustration.
- Text walls: children *scan*; long prose without structure loses them in seconds.
- Font sizes: 12 pt is the floor for older children (14 pt for younger); dyslexia-friendly defaults (sans-serif, no justified text, high contrast, A–/A+ size controls) are cheap and inclusive (ADHD/dyslexia are common in this age range) — with WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1).

**Why it happens:**
Designed on a desktop or in "mobile-ish" viewport by an adult; tested on nobody. Standalone-app mode (no browser chrome) is exactly the "app feel" the project wants — which is also precisely why in-app navigation is mandatory and easily forgotten.

**How to avoid:**
- Architect navigation first in the PWA/app-feel phase: persistent "⌂ Start / Zurück" affordance in the standalone app (top-left button + visible home link on every page), breadcrumb-free but button-led.
- Set touch-target minimums (≥ 48×48 CSS px with spacing) as a design-token rule in the UI phase; verify with a real 10–12 year old's hands.
- 200% zoom + 200% font-size must not break layout (WCAG 1.4.4): relative units, no fixed-width layout.
- Order: mobile-layout mockups => real-device test (daughter's phone) before content scale-up.

**Warning signs:**
- Mockups shown only in desktop viewport.
- No in-app back/start control in the mockup (because "the phone has a back button" — it won't, in standalone mode).
- Tap targets smaller than ~44 px in the design.

**Phase to address:** PWA "App feel" phase (in-app nav) + UI/design phase (targets, type scale) + real-device validation in Hosting/Launch.

## Technical Debt Patterns

| Shortcut | Immediate Benefit | Long-term Cost | When Acceptable |
|----------|-------------------|----------------|-----------------|
| No service worker (installed app = manifest + icon only) | Simpler, no cache bugs, iOS quirks avoided | No offline reading; fine, the goal is a facts site | Always acceptable here — offline is out of scope |
| Publishing content with hardcoded absolute URLs (for GitHub Pages subpath) | Quick first deploy | Breaks assets/icons if host/path changes; migration cost | Only for throwaway prototypes — never for the real site |
| Skipping manifest/icon polish in early phases | Faster to content | Child sees a gray-globe "broken" app icon; trust lost | Never — icon+manifest is a core "app feel" deliverable |
| Copying cookie-consent banner from template | Feels safe ("everyone has one") | Signals tracking that doesn't exist; erodes child's trust | Never — no cookies, no banner |
| Adding analytics "just to see visits" | Curiosity satisfaction, free-dashboards | GDPR duties, data of children, and it changes nothing about the product | Never — the site's promise is "tracked nothing" |
| Web-font from Google Fonts | Pretty typography quickly | Third-party request relays viewer IP + potential GDPR exposure + performance | Acceptable only if self-hosted after download; system-fonts are the simpler, safer default |
| Editing production content directly on the host's web UI | Instant fix | Drift between repo and live site; un-reproducible state | Only for one-off typos, immediately ported back to source |

## Integration Gotchas

| Integration | Common Mistake | Correct Approach |
|-------------|----------------|------------------|
| Web manifest (manifest.json) | Absolute paths / wrong `sizes` / missing icons; breaks on subpath hosts | Relative URLs from repo root; verify 512/192/180 px icons exist and match `sizes`; test installed-result visually |
| iOS "Add to Home Screen" | Assuming a prompt appears; expecting push/offline features that iOS-EU partially disables | Ship explicit install instructions page with screenshots for iOS + Android; don't rely on any capability that isn't silently degraded-safe |
| Static host CDN cache | Long `Cache-Control` on HTML → installed app never sees updates | HTML `no-cache`/`must-revalidate`; hash asset filenames; verify update propagation on the phone |
| GitHub Pages subpath | Start_url/icons 404 under `user.github.io/repo/` | Subpath-safe links from day one, or choose private-repo host (Cloudflare/Netlify) and dodge the subpath entirely |
| External links (to sources/other sites) | Clicking leaves the child's app/window without way back | `target="_blank" rel="noopener"`, and/or keep all content on-site: link only from a "Mehr wissen" section, never mid-page |
| Fonts/images from third-party CDNs | Unrequested external requests = privacy + ad-block appearance + loading failures | Self-host every asset; zero third-party requests is the compliance default |

## Performance Traps

| Trap | Symptoms | Prevention | When It Breaks |
|------|----------|------------|----------------|
| Heavy JS framework for interactive checks | Slow cold start on mid-range phone; janky taps | Static HTML + small vanilla JS; client-side only | Any mobile device with 2 GB RAM or mid-tier SoC — i.e. the daughter's phone |
| Large hero images / video | Multi-MB page on mobile data; slow install-to-first-use | <1 MB page budget; SVGs/optimized PNGs; no autoplaying video | On school/free Wi-Fi and slow home connections |
| Web-font + icon font loading | Render-blocking FOUT, extra requests, tracking lens | System fonts or self-hosted woff2 with `font-display: swap` | Old phones / slow networks — exactly the target audience |
| Unbounded DOM/single-page scrolling | Long list of all topics on one page → slow + overwhelming (a UX fail too) | Paginated topic cards (start page = grid of 6 topics) | Over ~50 elements; children lose orientation sooner than adults |
| Serving everything cache-first через SW | Stale content (see Pitfall 9) | No SW, or network-first for HTML | After any content update — immediately visible staleness |

Scale note: this site's realistic ceiling (daughter + class + friends) is tens to low hundreds of visits/month — bandwidth caps (100 GB/mo) are irrelevant. Do not optimize for scale that will never come; optimize for **cold-start speed on one mid-range phone** and **stale-content avoidance**.

## Security Mistakes

| Mistake | Risk | Prevention |
|---------|------|------------|
| Real child identity in repo/site (name, school, town, photos) | Permanent, indexable exposure of a minor; harassment/OSINT | Pseudonym on site; repo hygiene (no names/metadata/EXIF in assets, `.gitignore` drafts); parent's (not child's) data in Impressum |
| Third-party tracking/embeds (analytics, fonts, share widgets) | Gathers children's data → GDPR Art. 8 (consent age 16 DE) violations, IP leakage | Zero-third-party-requests rule; self-host all assets; no cookies |
| Pasting keys/URLs in env or repo | Any secret in a (possibly public) repo is compromised | No secrets exist in this project (static site) — keep it that way; never add a backend, forms, or API keys |
| Subdomain takeover (custom domain lapses) | Attacker claims name on host's wildcard domain | Keep DNS records pointed at host; renew domain; use host-managed domain if possible |
| Unsafe markdown/HTML in self-check content | XSS if content pipeline ever becomes CMS-ish | Static, human-authored files only; no user-supplied content ever (out of scope) |
| External images/scripts hotlinked | Malicious/inflated CDN content, tracking | No third-party references; all assets in-repo |

|---------|-------------|-----------------|
| Quiz scores/verdicts | Shame, anxiety, "I'm broken" narrative | Reflection prompts, no wrong answers, gentle mirrors + suggestions |
| Command-voice tips ("Du MUSST ihr Handy weglegen") | Feels like parent lecture; child tunes out | Invitation voice: "Probiere aus...", options and choice |
| No back-button in standalone app | Child gets stuck inside the app, gives up | Persistent in-app "Start/Zurück" affordance on every page |
| Text walls / adult German | Unreadable to a 10-year-old; scanning fails | ≤12-word sentences, bullets, bold key words, kid-examples |
| Babyish styling for a 12-year-old | Patronized; won't share with friends | Age-banded design: 9–12 wants "cool", not "cute"; it's fine to be minimal and modern |
| No feedback on taps / no progress indication | Feels broken or confusing | Visible tap states, page titles, "Topic 2 von 6" progress hints |
| Everything-danger tone | Dismissed as naive; loses credibility | Balance: "Was daran gut ist" per topic; myth-buster section |
| Icon/app look "broken" (globe) | Child won't open it as an app | Pixel-correct icons, real-device visual check at install-validation |

## "Looks Done But Isn't" Checklist

- [ ] **PWA/App feel:** Manifest exists AND has 512, 192, 180 px icons with matching `sizes` AND was visually verified on the daughter's actual phone (both iOS & Android paths) — not just "manifest returns 200".
- [ ] **Install path:** A step-by-step "App aufs Handy" page exists with screenshots and both OS paths; the daughter (or dad) actually followed it successfully end-to-end.
- [ ] **Updates:** Changed a real headline and verified the *installed app* shows the new text within a day (cache headers correct, no stale SW).
- [ ] **In-app navigation:** In standalone mode (no browser chrome), a back/start control exists and works from the deepest page.
- [ ] **German text quality:** No adult-formal phrasing, umlauts/''ß'' correct, no leftover English UI strings ("Back", "Start", "Next"), Impressum/Datenschutz present and reachable in ≤2 clicks.
- [ ] **Balance pass:** Every topic page has a "was gut daran ist" element; zero pages are pure fear.
- [ ] **Health claims:** Every claim attributed to the trusted set (AAP, Mayo, pediatric sleep sources) or phrased with appropriate hedges; the "myths" section separates fact from pop-science.
- [ ] **Touch/readability:** Tap targets ≥48 px, 12–14 pt base type, passes browser 200% zoom without breakage, WCAG contrast AA.
- [ ] **Privacy:** Zero third-party requests (check network tab from a fresh device — no googleapis, no fonts.gstatic.com, no analytics), no cookies, no child PII in repo or pages.
- [ ] **Shareability:** A friend can open the URL from a normal browser and everything works without install; nothing breaks if shared via WhatsApp link (no login wall, no "app required" lie).

## Recovery Strategies

| Pitfall | Recovery Cost | Recovery Steps |
|---------|---------------|----------------|
| Fear-mongering tone discovered late | MEDIUM | Targeted rewrite per topic with tone checklist; add balance sections; have daughter/peer review after |
| Stale installed app | LOW | Fix cache headers / version assets; tell user "remove app icon, re-add" once (or bump manifest) |
| Wrong health claim published | MEDIUM-HIGH | Fact-check gate catches most; if shipped, correct page + note "Hier war ein Fehler – danke fürs Aufpassen" (models good behavior!), re-deploy, re-verify installed device |
| Broken icon/manifest | LOW | Generate full icon set from SVG; re-deploy; re-add app on device; verify |
| Public repo exposing child data | HIGH | Rotate immediately: purge GitHub history (BFG) or migrate to private-repo host (Cloudflare/Netlify); scrub site pages; treat as incident |
| Installed app stuck in browser tab (iOS-EU edge cases) | LOW | Ship a bookmark-style fallback ("Füge sie als Lesezeichen hinzu – genauso gut") in install page so no user is left stranded |
| Text too hard for the audience | MEDIUM | Readability pass (sentence length, jargon) on flagged pages; test with one classmate |
